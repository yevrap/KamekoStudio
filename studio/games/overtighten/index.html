Overtighten

PROTOTYPE Seat every bolt inside its band — all of them at once. Turning any bolt loosens the ones it is coupled to, and past the strip point a thread is gone.

All three plates seated. That is every plate this experiment has — there is no progression layer behind it yet, on purpose.

What this is, and what it turned out to be

The first experiment built by the studio's own team. It was made to test one idea: that the interesting part of tightening a plate is the coupling between bolts, not the timing of a release — that working out the order would be the game.

It isn't, and the experiment is on the shelf saying so. Two independent reviews took the plates apart. Because loosening stops at zero, a bolt sitting at zero absorbs nothing, so in a single pass a bolt is only reduced by the neighbours turned after it — which makes each plate a back-substitution. Overshoot each bolt by the right amount and one hold per bolt clears it. Of the 146 ways to order the bolts across the three plates, 142 work; the four that don't miss a strip point by less than three units, which is a tuning accident rather than a puzzle. Going round the plate topping every bolt up, planning nothing at all, also clears every plate in at most three passes.

So what is here is a tactile convergence toy rather than the ordering puzzle it was meant to be. That is a real result, and the tests now assert it rather than the thing that was hoped for: a redesign is the work that flips them back.
