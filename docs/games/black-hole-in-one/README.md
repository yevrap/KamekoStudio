## What it is

Turn-based flick golf where every hole is a pocket planetary system. Pull back anywhere on the screen and release to fling a comet; the planets' gravity bends the shot the whole way. Sink the comet into the **black hole** — the cup — in as few flicks as you can. Holes are procedurally generated forever, each with a par; the score bar keeps a running total vs par, golf-style (E / +2 / −3).

Three things make a shot interesting:

- **Gravity is the club.** You rarely aim *at* the black hole — you aim where gravity will take the shot. On the first verification hole, every sinking solution aimed right of the target and let a planet's pull curve it in.
- **Planets are fairways.** A slow enough impact lands the comet on the planet's surface, and the next flick tees off from there. Long or blocked holes become hop-and-putt journeys.
- **The cup pulls back.** The black hole's own gravity funnels close shots in (like a golf cup's lip, but honest physics); a sunk comet spirals through the accretion ring. A 1-flick hole is a **BLACK HOLE IN ONE!**

Misses that fly out of bounds (or drift too long) are "lost in space": the comet returns to its last resting spot, the stroke already spent — no extra penalty. Repulsor pulsars (anti-gravity stars) start appearing from hole 6.

## Orbits (v27, BH-4)

A comet that sweeps around a planet **near-circularly** — moving nearly tangentially, at close to that radius's orbital speed, hugging the surface — is captured into a **live, station-kept orbit** instead of decaying, landing, or escaping. It circles indefinitely until you flick it out. The flick works from anywhere on screen (the game's normal drag-anywhere aim), and it's a **force-push impulse**: instead of launching from a standstill, the impulse is *added to the comet's current orbital velocity*, so a good release bends the motion you already have and slings you off with orbital speed for free. An orbit that happens to skim the black hole still sinks — an emergent, lucky ace.

Orbit sits deliberately *between* the two existing outcomes: slow tangential grazes still **land** (hop-and-putt), fast swings still **SLINGSHOT** away and escape; only genuine "swung around but stayed" passes are captured. Design spec and the decision trail: [Black Hole in One — Stable Orbits Exploration](../../archive/plans/black-hole-in-one-stable-orbits-exploration.md).

**Explore-only extension, 2026-07-18: tap to land or orbit.** In Explore, an inventory item (🧲 Orbit Magnet, ON by default) plus a tap gesture replaced the original "auto-capture + flick-at-the-planet" plan: tap a planet you're approaching → guaranteed scripted landing; tap the planet you're resting on → guaranteed scripted orbit launch; tap the planet you're orbiting → guaranteed landing; tap a black hole you're orbiting → warps to Town. Golf's strict `orbitCapture()` is untouched — this is purely an Explore traversal upgrade. Full trail: [Black Hole in One — Tap to Land or Orbit Build Plan (July 2026)](../../archive/plans/black-hole-in-one-tap-to-land-or-orbit-build-plan-july-2026.md), [Dev Log](../../archive/dev-logs/black-hole-in-one.md).

**Orbits do something, 2026-07-19: ✨ stardust rings (ORB-4).** ~25% of non-blackhole planets in Explore carry a gold pickup ring inside the orbit-capture band. Capturing onto one with Orbit Magnet ON snaps the orbit radius to the ring, so riding it out sweeps every dot within a revolution — orbiting stops being purely a landing detour and becomes a small reward loop of its own. Stardust pickups everywhere (not just on rings) now render gold instead of the fuel pickup's green, fixing a color bug that predates this item. Still open from the same arc: ORB-3 (station-orbit refuel trickle).

## Mid-flight pushes (OW-0, Explore only)

In Explore mode, the comet can be flicked **mid-flight** — you don't need to land or orbit first. The same drag-anywhere gesture works: touch anywhere to aim, pull back and release to add a force-push impulse to the comet's current velocity, bending its trajectory. The trail is preserved across pushes so your path reads as one continuous ribbon. A one-time "🚀 Mid-flight push!" toast fires on the first use per session.

This is **Explore-only** — golf modes keep the strict rest → aim → flight → rest loop. The feature extends the BH-4 force-push impulse model: the orbit flick-out already proved that adding to velocity (instead of overwriting) feels physical and rewards the player's existing momentum. Mid-flight pushes take that to its logical conclusion for freeform exploration. Freeze-on-aim (comet holds position while dragging) was added post-ship for accuracy, but is toggleable in the Game Menu for players who prefer the live urgency of aiming a moving target.

## Town Shop — meta-progression (EXP-1, complete July 16, 2026)

Explore mode now has a persistent reason to keep flying and a reason to go home. **Stardust** (a collectible pickup, alongside fuel) survives reloads (`blackHoleInOne_stardust`) and buys permanent upgrades at **Town** — resting the comet on the tee rock pops a bottom-sheet shop panel (reusing the editor/custom-map bar chrome) with three items, each a shared 15/35/60✨ cost curve across 3 tiers:

- **⛽ Fuel Tank** — max fuel 100 → 130 → 160 → 200. Purchases add the capacity *delta* to current fuel rather than topping off to full (a deliberate call — see [Black Hole in One — Explore Progression (Stardust Upgrades)](../../archive/plans/black-hole-in-one-explore-progression-stardust-upgrades.md) for why).
- **🌀 Fuel Siphon** — fuel gained per pickup, +20 → +28 → +36 → +45.
- **📡 Long-Range Sensor** — the active-chunk load radius around the camera, 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 chunks — higher tiers reveal bodies and pickups farther out.

All three are **Explore-only**; Endless keeps its original fuel system completely untouched so high-score runs stay comparable session to session. Town itself now also has a **visual identity**: the tee rock gets a distinct pulsing warm-gold halo and surface ring (Explore only — golf/editor tees stay plain), so arriving reads as a place rather than a coordinate the shop happens to pop up at. Full design history, the shop-panel data-driven refactor (EXP-1b.5), and the Sensor reframe (the original "hazard visibility" idea had nothing to attach to — no hazards exist in Explore) are in [Black Hole in One — Explore Progression (Stardust Upgrades)](../../archive/plans/black-hole-in-one-explore-progression-stardust-upgrades.md). This closes the arc — nothing open.

## How it pauses (relaxed at v27)

Originally pause-proof **by construction** — between flicks the game was completely at rest (Q2=B). BH-4's live orbits knowingly relax that: an orbit is motion between flicks. OW-0's mid-flight pushes extend this further — flight is now continuous in Explore mode. **Yev's ruling (July 15): that's fine — auto-pause is enough, and live/complicated motion is the point.** The sim still auto-pauses on tab switch / window blur / drawer open and resumes cleanly (flight and orbit both freeze via the `S.paused` gate in the main loop), so being dropped mid-motion still costs nothing. **Decorative moons (OW-5, 2026-07-17)** are the first idle background motion in the game — safe because they're Explore-only render decoration with zero gravity/collision, so golf's "everything frozen at rest" pillar (where idle motion was originally cut for) never actually sees one.

## Design decisions and why

- **Original hooks, not a Desert Golfing reskin (the Flow Glider lesson).** The design source cited Desert Golfing / Angry Birds for the *turn-based flick* feel only. Nothing else is lifted: radial-gravity planet-hopping, the black-hole cup with capture spiral, aim-with-gravity solutions, slingshot celebration, and pulsars are this game's own. There is no terrain scroll, no destruction, no slingshot-bird framing.
- **One screen per hole, no camera.** Every hole fits the portrait viewport. Zero camera work, always phone-comfortable, and it makes each hole read like a little diorama.
- **Short honest trajectory preview (~half a second of real simulation).** Desert Golfing gives you nothing; a full-path preview would make the game play itself. The short preview teaches how gravity grabs the shot without solving the hole for you. Preview length is a playtest question.
- **Drag anywhere, not on the comet.** Your thumb never covers the ball; drag direction is pull-back slingshot style (drag down → fling up), with a power arrow that shifts green → gold → red.
- **Friendly out-of-bounds rule.** Golf's stroke-and-distance felt punishing at phone-session stakes; here a lost shot just returns you to your last spot. The wasted flick is the penalty.
- **Orbit traps decay by "space dust."** After ~9 s of flight, a gentle drag ramps in, so a comet caught in a loop spirals into *something* (often the hole — feels like fate) instead of teleporting or orbiting forever. Hard cap at 24 s.
- **Par comes from blockers, not distance:** par 2 base, +1 with 3+ planets, +1 with a pulsar, cap 4. Early holes are birdie-able; aces stay possible and celebrated.
- **2D canvas (Q6=C per-game call).** The cool factor is trails, the rotating accretion ring, influence rings, nebula gradients, and particle bursts — none of it needs Three.js.
- **Tiny synthesized sound** (flick / bounce / land / sink / fanfare via WebAudio oscillators) — no assets, no network.

## How it was verified (beyond eyeballing)

`node --test` and the 17-page `npm run smoke` suite green; live playtest in a mobile-size browser plus **in-page physics sims through the real game pipeline**: a coarse aim-search found 28 sinking solutions on hole 1 (healthy aim window, all curving around the guard planet); a real shot sank with the capture spiral and scored PAR correctly; a lob at a planet landed and re-teed (hop-and-putt works); an overshot slingshotted past the hole, went out of bounds, and returned to the tee with the stroke kept. ## Stability sprint (July 16, 2026)

After promotion the game took 13 feature commits in a day across three arcs at once (Orbits, Map Maker, Open World) and started to feel out of control. Yev called a **stability sprint** — freeze features, fix what's broken, then resume one arc at a time. Full trail: [Black Hole in One — Stability Sprint (July 2026)](../../archive/plans/black-hole-in-one-stability-sprint-july-2026.md). Six items shipped:

- **Liftoff grace (STAB-1).** A comet resting on a big planet could get dragged straight back and re-land in place. For ~0.35 s after flicking off a planet's surface, *only that planet's* pull is damped (ramping back to full), so the shot reliably gets clear. Decision D1=C: keep big planets, no size cap, no new mechanic. Every other body's gravity is untouched.
- **Temporary zoom-out (STAB-2, decision D2=b).** Orbiting or hugging a body too big to read at the normal scale (Explore's giant planets) gently zooms the view out to fit, then snaps back. **Golf modes never trigger it** — their bodies always fit — so the one-screen, no-camera pillar stays intact.
- **Map Maker mobile fix (STAB-3).** Root cause of the flaky drag/delete was a missing `sfx.pop()` (add/delete threw) and a `pointercancel` handler that couldn't clear the drag — either one wedged the editor. Fixed both; the editor never wedges now.
- **Menu declutter + shared-map arrival (STAB-4/5).** The "Play Shared Map" buttons had been showing on the *normal* menu the whole time (a missing `.hidden` CSS rule). Now the shared-map card appears only for `?map=` links, in plain language ("Someone shared a custom map with you" / "Play this map" / "No thanks"), and mode select is grouped into **Play** and **Make & explore**. Explore stays a first-class mode (D3=c).
- **Coverage (STAB-6).** Tests 27 → 256, including a map serialize→deserialize round-trip so shared links can't silently corrupt.

Verified in good shape at close: hole 1 sinks on 51/300 tee shots (no soft-lock), golf never zooms, zero console errors. **Freeze lifted.**

- **🌀 OW-3 — black-hole wormhole → Town → return portal, shipped 2026-07-17.** `getChunkBodies()` seeds a `type: 'blackhole'` body into roughly 1 in 25 chunks (fixed r=22, mass on the same `m=r²` density convention every other body uses — that alone keeps its surface gravity under `THRUST_A` with more margin than a Giant gets, no special-casing needed). It sits in `world.bodies`, so it pulls the comet with real gravity like a planet, but a proximity check in `explore.step()` (radius comfortably bigger than `stepBody()`'s own collision radius, so it always fires first) triggers a warp instead of a landing: the comet spirals into the black hole over ~1 s — visually the same inward-spiral pattern as golf's cup capture, a fresh implementation rather than a shared function, since Explore has no strokes/par/scorecard for `holeComplete()` to report — then lands on the tee rock exactly like flying there directly (EXP-1, unchanged). A **🌀 Return Portal** row appears in the Town Shop panel once a warp has happened, sending the comet back to the exact black hole + spot it came from (bookmarked in `blackHoleInOne_exploreHome`, survives reload), nudged just outside the warp radius along the original approach direction so arriving back doesn't instantly re-trigger it. `physics.js`'s golf `blackHole` param — a single slot backing ~300 tests — is untouched; this is entirely Explore's own bookkeeping. 11 new unit tests, full suite green (314), golf/editor verified byte-identical.

Rolled last in the chunk generator's rng stream so it perturbs no other body odds. 6 new unit tests, full suite green (320).
